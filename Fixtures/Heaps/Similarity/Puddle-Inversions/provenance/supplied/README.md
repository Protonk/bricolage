# Puddle Inversions — web-agent candidate handoff

**Prepared:** 2 November 2026  
**Requested future grouping:** approximately 35–50 images  
**Supplied:** 45 candidate *file-page leads*; 19 individual Commons pages text-checked, 26 identified only from category listing; zero image files acquired.  
**Intended project:** Bricolage, similarity heap `Puddle-Inversions`  
**Media:** still photographs only; no video or animated imagery. JPEG and JPEG-like file titles only in this handoff.

## Brief and inclusion principle

Collect still images in which a puddle or similarly small, localized, rain- or surface-water accumulation **reflects some part of a scene**. The interest is the potential for inverted, duplicated, displaced, or fragmented scenery inside the picture. The reflection need not dominate. Architecture, vehicles, people, plants, animals, roads, distant skies, incidental puddles, poor light, unusual orientation, low resolution, and everyday compositions are all welcome.

**This is a loose visual selection, not a claim of intra-heap homogeneity.** Avoid pursuing only elegant near-symmetrical photographs; leave rough edges. No crop, tonal normalization, or downstream similarity rule is proposed. An ordinary scene with a barely consequential puddle can be kept if the image is otherwise interesting. This is not a published-collection-membership fixture: Commons category membership is an initial discovery method, not the definition of the final heap.

Exclusions for this first pass: animated GIFs, videos, frame extracts from motion media, illustrative graphics posing as still photographs, images without a genuinely visible puddle/reflection on later review, and clearly redundant variants of the same capture when they crowd out other scenes. The pictured subjects themselves may move; this restriction concerns the **media**. An image of a moving tram is still a photograph.

## Recoverable bounded starting population

**Primary source:** [Wikimedia Commons, `Category:Reflections in puddles`](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Reflections_in_puddles).

The web-accessible category view checked on 2026-11-02 reported **432 direct files**, with **200 displayed on its alphabetically first page**, and two subcategories: `Reflections in puddles by country` and `Sunlight reflections from puddles`. The view was cached (crawled approximately three weeks earlier), so 432 is a **page-observed count**, not a verified real-time count on 2 November. All 45 candidate names in this handoff were visible on that first page, and **no subcategories were traversed for selection**. This is deliberately a bounded, non-exhaustive seed, not an exhaustive export of 432. There is no year, country, upload-date, license, or image-rating filter. Only still-image filename extensions `.jpg`, `.JPG`, and `.jpeg` were selected, with other media formats left out.

The individual file-page URLs in `candidates.csv` preserve the exact proposed identities, independent of future category membership changes. A suggested current-population API retrieval is described in `source-recovery.md` (not executed or verified here). No original downloaded source export was available.

## Selection and review actually performed

The direct category file listing and file titles/dimensions were read. **Nineteen individual file pages were opened and their textual descriptions, authorship, and licensing inspected.** Their recorded author and license fields are evidence *from those pages*, not my own determination of legal status. **Twenty-six entries were taken from the category listing only**; rights for these are marked `Not checked`. Some title-based rationales are intentionally tentative and require visual confirmation. Individual thumbnails, rendered images, and original image bytes were **not examined**, and no file was downloaded. A file-page listing on Wikimedia Commons is not itself proof that reuse terms suit a particular downstream publication.

The page-checked entries include common CC BY-SA terms, several CC BY entries, a German CC BY 3.0 variant, and one historical institutional upload displaying Flickr Commons **"no known copyright restrictions"** rather than a conclusive public-domain determination (`PI-20`). Several photographers add specific attribution wording or requests on their file pages; these notes are retained next to the corresponding candidates. An agent integrating images must re-verify each page and applicable terms.

## Why this mix?

The prospective scenes range from rain-soaked urban streets and historical architecture to woodland paths, rural tracks, a bird, sports scenes, a port, aircraft, a plant, a cyclist, a photographer, and ordinary parking areas. There are day/night, color/historical, large/small, incidental/prominent and wide/tall/square formats. Those distinctions arise from titles and page text; they are **not visual assessments of the photographs**.

Possible awkward members should not be discarded to make the heap look consistent. Some candidates may prove visually unsuitable; `PI-09`, `PI-27`, and `PI-28`, for example, have evocative but uninformative titles, and `PI-43` might depict a plant in water without a strong inversion. Their inclusion *as leads* expresses the loose boundary rather than certainty.

## Related captures and preselected rejects

The category contains visible same-place series (e.g., four `Cot Way` files, three `Boulevard du Musée...feu...` files, two `Frobisher Crescent` files), and some candidate siblings. To avoid allowing a small number of sites or derivatives to dominate this initial population, one representative was used where clear titles suggested series, while others were withheld. These are *selection choices*, not judgments of visual redundancy after inspecting pixels. See `preselected-rejects.md` for exact filenames and rationales. The cropped derivative of `Puddle Hopping.jpg` is explicitly documented on that file page.

## Open questions and limitations

The true amount and nature of reflection in the 26 listing-only leads is unknown. The 19 page-checked leads have descriptions and reuse notices but were not visually inspected. Nothing has been checked against existing local Bricolage byte hashes, because only a condensed list of heap slugs/counts was supplied. The 432 figure may change. The 1948 sports image (`PI-20`) has a different rights-evidence regime from standard CC-licensed author uploads and deserves extra attention. The collection can be thinned later, but no claim is made that these 45 would survive unchanged.
