# Thumbnail-review instructions

The local reviewer must have **the actual preview bytes**. A Commons category or filename is a lead, never proof of visual suitability. Work in short batches, for example 20 previews on a 4×5 contact sheet. Keep the preview ID alongside every image and inspect larger previews only for ambiguous cases.

For each candidate record `accept`, `reject`, or `defer`, one concise *observable* reason, the candidate ID, preview filename/hash, and any possibly related candidate IDs. Keep `pending_visual_review` for genuinely unviewed media. Visual scope decisions, work/provenance identity, rights review, and transport status are separate axes.

These are **Curatorial** collections, not Similarity prompt heaps. Keep legitimate unusual examples. Do **not** use visual scores, center/border/outlier labels, target-aware screening, subject-bucket balancing, or an effort to make all 50 pictures look alike.

## Rocks in Thin Section — `Rocks-in-Thin-Section`

Eligible: identifiable **photomicrographs of prepared rock thin sections** at microscope scale, in PPL, XPL or other documented microscopy arrangements. Distinct color palettes, crystal grain sizes, dark fields and foliation are all fine. Multiple mineral types are welcome.

Thumbnail outliers to reject: a landscape/outcrop photo, hand specimen, geology diagram, a photograph of an entire slide without a micrographic view, or a screenshot whose thin-section image is only a tiny inset. Check that the microstructure dominates; don't reject simply because it is monochrome or poorly saturated.

Relatedness: names containing PPL/XPL, plane/crossed polars, same sample number, and magnification may indicate multiple optical views of the same slide or capture. Record evidence and generally select one where the same specimen/view is identifiable; if uncertain, `defer`, do not pretend different polarizations prove independent sources.

## Stone Hand Axes — `Stone-Hand-Axes`

Eligible: photographs of prehistoric stone hand axes/bifaces and **drawings or historical archaeological illustrations** of them. A page with more than one clearly depicted hand axe can be eligible. Object silhouettes can be irregular, damaged or ambiguous; graphic medium and background are not disqualifiers.

Thumbnail outliers to reject: modern hafted axes, metal hatchets, modern replicas offered as originals without documentation, generic flint flakes with no hand-axe context, unrelated human portraits, museum-wide scenes where the artifact is merely a tiny element, or diagrams with no actual axe representation. Some drawings in the named category may be cleavers or manufacturing schematics rather than hand axes—review individually.

Relatedness: detect alternative object views (front/back, left/right), repeated museum accession IDs, and a drawing/photograph of the **same known artifact**. Check metadata before excluding. Do not assert that visually similar archaeological tools are the same artifact.

## Ex Libris — `Ex-Libris`

Eligible: identifiable bookplates or printed ownership emblems, isolated or still on a book page where the bookplate is prominent. Engraved heraldry, woodcuts, ornamental texts and modern graphic approaches are all in scope, including nineteenth- and twentieth-century examples.

Thumbnail outliers to reject: unrelated book-cover photography, portraits or title pages without a readable/recognizable ownership mark, photographs of exhibitions where the bookplate is too small to inspect, and illustrations unrelated to a bookplate. A complicated or minimalist design is still eligible when it is clearly an ex libris.

Relatedness: repeated scans of the same printed plate, restored crops, changes in contrast/color, editions and alternate file encodings. Preserve printed-work keys and evidence; different bookplates by the same artist or owner are not automatically duplicates.

## Decisions and uncertainty

Use short reasons like “reject: outcrop landscape, no microscope field”, “accept: biface drawing fills image”, or “defer: apparent PPL/XPL pair with rm-00xx”. Do not turn a visual objection into fabricated provenance. Unknown author and exact specimen IDs stay null. If the image cannot be decoded/viewed, mark preview transport failure, not visual rejection.
